RequestFox vs Hoppscotch

Pick Hoppscotch if you want an open-source API client on the web, desktop or CLI that your team can self-host, with WebSocket, SSE, Socket.IO and MQTT clients [3]. Pick RequestFox to record and judge the calls your own web app makes while you test it. RequestFox imports Hoppscotch collections.

Facts about Hoppscotch are from its own documentation, checked 4 November 2026; sources below. Written by the RequestFox team, for RequestFox 2.5.0.

Where Hoppscotch is stronger

  • Open source, and you can self-host it for a team [1].
  • An MQTT client [3]. RequestFox has none.
  • It runs on the web with nothing to install [1].

Where RequestFox is different

  • It records what your app actually sends and flags the problems, each tied to the click that caused it.
  • Mock and fault rules change the responses your app receives.
  • History keeps every session, so you can compare runs and export evidence for a bug report.

Sockets and streams in the browser

While you record, RequestFox lists the page's own WebSocket frames and server-sent events next to its other calls, and reads Socket.IO frames as events with their arguments. Its WebSocket client connects to any ws:// or wss:// server, has a Socket.IO mode, and keeps the messages you save.
