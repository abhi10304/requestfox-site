RequestFox vs Hoppscotch

Pick Hoppscotch for an open-source API client on the web, desktop or CLI that teams can self-host, with WebSocket, SSE, Socket.IO and MQTT clients [3]. Pick RequestFox to record and judge the calls your own web app makes while you test it. RequestFox imports Hoppscotch collections.

Facts about Hoppscotch are from its own documentation, checked 1 November 2026; sources below. Written by the RequestFox team, for RequestFox 2.3.0.

Where Hoppscotch is stronger

  • Open source, and you can self-host it for a team [1].
  • An MQTT client, which RequestFox does not have [3].
  • Runs on the web with no install at all [1].

Where RequestFox is different

  • It records what your app really sends and flags the problems, tied to your click.
  • Mock and fault rules change the answers your app receives.
  • History keeps every session to compare runs, and exports evidence for bug reports.

Sockets and streams in the browser

While recording, RequestFox lists the page's own WebSocket frames and server-sent events next to its other calls, and reads Socket.IO frames as events with their arguments. Its WebSocket client connects to any ws:// or wss:// server, with a Socket.IO mode, and keeps messages you save.
